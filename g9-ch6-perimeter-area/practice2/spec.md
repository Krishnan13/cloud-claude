## PART 1 (Q1-Q5)

Q1. A circular lotus pond in a temple garden is fenced once with 132 m of wire. Draw a rough diagram.
 (a) Find the radius of the pond.
 (b) A second fence is put up 7 m outside the first fence, all the way round. Find how much more wire the second fence needs than the first.
 (c) Another pond has a radius of 35 m. It also gets a second fence 7 m outside the first one. Will the extra wire needed be more than, less than, or the same as your answer to (b)? Explain.
ANSWER: (a) 2 x 22/7 x r = 132, so r = 21 m. (b) New radius 28 m; circumference = 2 x 22/7 x 28 = 176 m; extra = 176 - 132 = 44 m. (c) The same, 44 m. Extra wire = 2 x pi x (R + 7) - 2 x pi x R = 2 x pi x 7 = 44 m, whatever R is. (Check: 2 x 22/7 x 42 - 2 x 22/7 x 35 = 264 - 220 = 44.)

Q2. Say whether each statement is true or false. Give a reason for each answer.
 (a) The value of pi is exactly 22/7.
 (b) For every circle, the circumference divided by the diameter gives the same number.
 (c) Arjun measures a bangle. Its circumference is 22 cm and its diameter is 7 cm. Arjun says, "This proves that pi is exactly 22/7."
ANSWER: (a) False. pi is irrational, so it cannot be written as a fraction of two whole numbers. 22/7 is a close approximation (22/7 = 3.142..., pi = 3.14159...). (b) True. This constant ratio is the number pi. (c) False. Every measurement is approximate, so a measurement cannot prove an exact value. Also pi is irrational, so it cannot equal 22/7.
[fig fc] a circle with diameter d and "circumference C" marked; caption "C / d = pi for every circle".

Q3. A railway line goes round a curve. The curve is an arc of a circle of radius 210 m.
 (a) A train goes round the curve and turns through an angle of 60 degrees. Draw a rough diagram. Find the distance the train travels on the curve.
 (b) On another curve of the same radius, a train travels 330 m. Find the angle through which the train turns.
ANSWER: (a) arc = 60/360 x 2 x 22/7 x 210 = 1/6 x 1320 = 220 m. (b) full circumference = 1320 m; 330/1320 = 1/4, so angle = 1/4 x 360 = 90 degrees.
[fig fc] arc with centre O, radius 210 m, angle 60 degrees, arc length 220 m.

Q4. In a 200 m race on a school track, each runner runs one bend and one straight. The bend in every lane is a semicircle. Each lane is 1.4 m wide. All runners finish at the same line. Assume that each runner runs along the inner edge of the lane. Draw a rough diagram of lanes 1 and 2.
 (a) Explain why the runner in lane 2 must start ahead of the runner in lane 1.
 (b) Find how far ahead of the lane 1 runner the lane 2 runner must start.
 (c) Find how far ahead of the lane 1 runner the lane 4 runner must start.
 (d) Ravi says, "If the track had a bigger bend, the answer to (b) would change." Is Ravi right? Explain.
ANSWER: (a) The straights are equal, but the bend in lane 2 has a larger radius, so it is longer. To make every runner run 200 m, the outer runner starts ahead. (b) Radius of lane 2 bend = r + 1.4. Extra length = 22/7 x (r + 1.4) - 22/7 x r = 22/7 x 1.4 = 4.4 m. (c) Lane 4 radius = r + 3 x 1.4 = r + 4.2; extra = 22/7 x 4.2 = 13.2 m (= 3 x 4.4). (d) No. The difference is pi x 1.4 = 4.4 m. The radius r cancels, so the stagger does not depend on the size of the bend.
[fig fc] two concentric semicircles (inner radius r, outer r + 1.4 m) joined to a straight, with start marks; label "1.4 m" between lanes.

Q5. A garden bed ABCD is shaped like a parallelogram. AB = 18 m and AD = 12 m. The perpendicular distance between the sides AB and DC is 10 m.
 (a) Draw a rough diagram. Mark all the lengths and show the 10 m distance with a dotted line.
 (b) Raju finds the area like this: Area = 18 x 12 = 216 m^2. Explain Raju's mistake. Find the correct area of the bed.
 (c) Find the perpendicular distance between the sides AD and BC.
ANSWER: (a) diagram. (b) Raju multiplied the two sides. The area of a parallelogram is base x the perpendicular height on that base, and 12 m is a slanting side, not the height. Area = 18 x 10 = 180 m^2. (c) 12 x h = 180, so h = 15 m.

## PART 2 (Q6-Q10)

Q6. A strip of cloth has two straight parallel edges that are 7 cm apart. Kavi draws two triangles, PQR and SQR, on the strip. Both triangles have the side QR = 12 cm along the lower edge. The points P and S are on the upper edge. In triangle PQR, the angles at Q and R are acute. In triangle SQR, the angle at Q is obtuse.
 (a) Draw both triangles in one rough diagram. Show the height of each triangle with a dotted line.
 (b) Find the area of each triangle.
 (c) Priya says, "Triangle SQR has the larger area, because its sides are longer." Is Priya right? Explain.
ANSWER: (b) Each area = 1/2 x 12 x 7 = 42 cm^2. For SQR the height falls outside the triangle, on RQ extended, but it is still 7 cm. (c) No. Area depends on the base and the perpendicular height, not on the slanting sides. Both have base 12 cm and height 7 cm (the distance between the parallel edges), so both areas are 42 cm^2.
[fig fc] two horizontal parallel lines 7 cm apart; Q, R on lower line (QR = 12 cm); P above between Q and R; S above, to the LEFT of Q, so angle SQR is obtuse; dotted perpendicular from S to RQ extended with right-angle mark; dotted height from P.

Q7. A glass panel ABCD in a lamp is shaped like a parallelogram, and its area is 96 cm^2. Two thin metal strips run along the diagonals AC and BD. They meet at O and divide the glass into four triangular pieces. Draw a rough diagram.
 (a) Explain why triangles AOB and BOC have equal areas. (Hint: what is BO in triangle ABC?)
 (b) Explain why all four pieces have equal areas. Find the area of each piece.
 (c) Neha says, "The four pieces are congruent." Is Neha right? Explain.
ANSWER: (a) The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so O is the midpoint of AC. So BO is a median of triangle ABC. A median divides a triangle into two triangles of equal area, so ar(AOB) = ar(BOC). (b) In the same way, CO is a median of triangle BCD (O is the midpoint of BD), so ar(BOC) = ar(COD); and DO is a median of triangle CDA, so ar(COD) = ar(DOA). So all four are equal. Each = 96 / 4 = 24 cm^2. (c) Not in general. AOB is congruent to COD, and BOC is congruent to DOA (SAS: OA = OC, OB = OD, vertically opposite angles). But AOB and BOC have different sides when AB is not equal to BC, so they are not congruent. They are all congruent only when ABCD is a rhombus. Equal area does not mean congruent.
[fig fc] a slanted parallelogram (sides clearly unequal, e.g. A(0,0) B(4,0) C(5.2,2) D(1.2,2)) with both diagonals, O marked, the four triangles in alternating light/no grey fill.

Q8. A triangular plot ABC has sides BC = 90 m, CA = 100 m and AB = 170 m.
 (a) Find the area of the plot using Heron's formula.
 (b) Use your answer to (a) to find the shortest distance from the corner A to the line BC.
 (c) Draw a rough diagram showing this shortest distance AD. Does D lie on the side BC, or on BC extended beyond C? Find CD and check your answer to (b) using AB.
ANSWER: (a) s = (90 + 100 + 170)/2 = 180. s-a = 90, s-b = 80, s-c = 10. Area = sqrt(180 x 90 x 80 x 10) = sqrt(12960000) = 3600 m^2. (b) 1/2 x 90 x AD = 3600, so AD = 80 m. (c) Angle C is obtuse, so D lies on BC extended beyond C. In right triangle ADC: CD = sqrt(100^2 - 80^2) = sqrt(3600) = 60 m. BD = 90 + 60 = 150 m. In right triangle ADB: AB = sqrt(150^2 + 80^2) = sqrt(28900) = 170 m. This matches, so AD = 80 m is correct.
[fig fc] B(0,0), C(90,0), D(150,0), A(150,80) scale 0.04 cm per m; BC solid, CD dashed; AD dotted with right-angle mark; labels 90 m, 100 m, 170 m, 80 m, 60 m.

Q9. A garden bed is shaped like an isosceles triangle. Its perimeter is 64 m and its unequal side (the base) is 24 m. Draw a rough diagram.
 (a) Find the length of each equal side.
 (b) Find the area of the bed using Heron's formula.
 (c) Find the area again in a different way, using the height on the base. Check that both answers agree.
ANSWER: (a) (64 - 24)/2 = 20 m. (b) s = 32; s-a = 12, s-b = 12, s-c = 8. Area = sqrt(32 x 12 x 12 x 8) = sqrt(36864) = 192 m^2. (c) The height from the top corner meets the base at its midpoint (12 m from each end). Height = sqrt(20^2 - 12^2) = sqrt(256) = 16 m. Area = 1/2 x 24 x 16 = 192 m^2. Both agree.
[fig fc] isosceles triangle base 24, sides 20, dotted height 16 with right-angle mark, base halves 12 and 12.

Q10. [fig student] A plot of land ABCD has all four corners on a circle, as shown. AB = 25 m, BC = 60 m, CD = 52 m and DA = 39 m.
 (a) Find the area of the plot using Brahmagupta's formula.
 (b) The diagonal AC is a diameter of the circle, and AC = 65 m. So the angles at B and D are right angles. Find the area of the plot again by splitting it along AC into two triangles. Check that it agrees with (a).
 (c) A rectangle 12 m by 5 m also has all four corners on a circle. Use Brahmagupta's formula to find its area. Does the formula give the answer you expect? Explain.
ANSWER: (a) s = (25 + 60 + 52 + 39)/2 = 88. s-a = 63, s-b = 28, s-c = 36, s-d = 49. Area = sqrt(63 x 28 x 36 x 49) = sqrt(3111696) = 1764 m^2. (b) ar(ABC) = 1/2 x 25 x 60 = 750 m^2; ar(ACD) = 1/2 x 39 x 52 = 1014 m^2; total 1764 m^2. Agrees. (c) s = (12 + 5 + 12 + 5)/2 = 17; (17-12)(17-5)(17-12)(17-5) = 5 x 12 x 5 x 12 = 3600; area = sqrt(3600) = 60 m^2 = 12 x 5. Yes: for a rectangle the formula gives sqrt(b x l x b x l) = l x b.

## PART 3 (Q11-Q15)

Q11. Long ago, Baudhayana (write: Baudh\={a}yana) showed how to change a rectangle into a square of the same area. Follow his steps for a rectangular field 18 m long and 8 m wide. Draw a diagram for each step.
 (a) Cut off a square of side 8 m from one end of the rectangle. What are the length and breadth of the strip that is left?
 (b) Cut the strip along its length into two equal strips. Place one strip along the right side of the square and the other along the top of the square. This makes an L-shape. Show that the L-shape fits inside a square of side 13 m, with a small square missing at one corner. Find the side of the missing square.
 (c) Find the area of the L-shape in two ways: from the rectangle, and as (big square) - (small square).
 (d) Baudhayana now draws a right triangle with hypotenuse 13 m and one side 5 m. Find the third side. Explain why a square on this third side has the same area as the field.
ANSWER: (a) 10 m by 8 m. (b) Each new strip is 5 m by 8 m. The square becomes 8 + 5 = 13 m on each side, except the 5 m by 5 m corner, which is empty. Missing square side = 5 m. (c) Rectangle: 18 x 8 = 144 m^2. Squares: 13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144 m^2. (d) Third side = sqrt(13^2 - 5^2) = sqrt(144) = 12 m. By Pythagoras, (third side)^2 = 13^2 - 5^2 = area of the L-shape = area of the field. So the square of side 12 m has area 144 m^2, the same as the field.
[fig fc] three small figures side by side: (1) 18 by 8 rectangle split into an 8 by 8 square and a 10 by 8 strip; (2) the L-shape inside a 13 by 13 square with the 5 by 5 corner hatched/dotted as missing; (3) right triangle with sides 13, 5, 12.

Q12. The top of a round table has a circumference of 220 cm.
 (a) Find the radius of the table top.
 (b) Find the area of the table top.
 (c) A bigger round table has a radius twice as long. Ravi says, "Its top has twice the area." Is Ravi right? Find the area of the bigger table top and explain.
ANSWER: (a) 2 x 22/7 x r = 220, r = 35 cm. (b) 22/7 x 35 x 35 = 3850 cm^2. (c) No. New radius 70 cm; area = 22/7 x 70 x 70 = 15400 cm^2 = 4 x 3850. When the radius doubles, r x r becomes 4 times as large, so the area becomes 4 times, not 2 times.
[fig fc] two circles, radius 35 cm and 70 cm.

 (a) Draw a rough diagram. Shade the part of the glass that the blade wipes.
 (b) Find the area of the glass that the blade wipes.
 (c) Find the length of the path of the tip of the arm.
ANSWER: (b) Area = big sector - small sector = 90/360 x 22/7 x (21 x 21 - 7 x 7) = 1/4 x 22/7 x 392 = 308 cm^2. (Big sector 346.5, small 38.5: do NOT show these decimals; use the factored form above.) (c) 90/360 x 2 x 22/7 x 21 = 33 cm.

Q14. [fig student] A floor tile ABCD is a square of side 14 cm. Two quarter circles are drawn inside it: one with centre A and radius AB, and one with centre C and radius CB. Both arcs go from B to D. The leaf-shaped part between the two arcs is shaded.
 (a) Find the area of the shaded leaf.
 (b) Find the perimeter of the shaded leaf.
 (c) Meena finds the area of the leaf like this: (area of one quarter circle) + (area of the other quarter circle) - (area of the square). Explain why her method works, and check that it gives the same answer.
ANSWER: (a) The diagonal BD cuts the leaf into two equal segments. One segment = quarter circle - triangle ABD = 1/4 x 22/7 x 14 x 14 - 1/2 x 14 x 14 = 154 - 98 = 56 cm^2. Leaf = 2 x 56 = 112 cm^2. (b) Each arc = 1/4 x 2 x 22/7 x 14 = 22 cm; perimeter = 44 cm. (c) The two quarter circles together cover the whole square, and they overlap exactly in the leaf. So the leaf is counted twice: 154 + 154 = square + leaf; leaf = 308 - 196 = 112 cm^2. Same answer.

Q15. [fig student] A square garden has side 28 m. At each corner there is a flower bed shaped like a quarter circle of radius 14 m, with its centre at the corner of the garden. The rest of the garden is lawn.
 (a) Find the area of the lawn.
 (b) The lawn has a curved border. Find the length of this border.
 (c) The gardener makes the flower beds smaller, with radius 7 m. Find the new area of the lawn and the new length of its border.
 (d) Did the lawn's border get longer or shorter in (c)? Explain why.
ANSWER: (a) Four quarter circles = one full circle of radius 14 = 22/7 x 14 x 14 = 616 m^2. Lawn = 28 x 28 - 616 = 784 - 616 = 168 m^2. (b) Border = four quarter arcs = one full circumference = 2 x 22/7 x 14 = 88 m. (c) Beds = one circle of radius 7 = 154 m^2; lawn = 784 - 154 = 630 m^2. Border = four quarter arcs (one circle, 2 x 22/7 x 7 = 44 m) + four straight pieces of 28 - 7 - 7 = 14 m each (56 m) = 100 m. (d) Longer (100 m > 88 m). With radius 14 m the beds touch, so the border is only arcs. With radius 7 m, the border has shorter arcs but also gains four straight pieces along the sides of the square.
