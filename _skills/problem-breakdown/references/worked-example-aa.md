# Worked example: the AA similarity worksheet

Source: `assets/aa-similarity.tex` (Grade 10, NCERT Chapter 6, Section 6.4).

## Target problem (Question 9, exam wording)

In triangle ABC, angle ABC = 90° and BD is perpendicular to AC, with D on AC.
(i) Prove that triangle ADB ~ triangle BDC. (ii) Hence prove that BD² = AD × DC.
(iii) If AD = 4 cm and DC = 9 cm, find BD and the area of triangle ABC.

## Idea list (from solving the target)

1. Angle sum: in triangle ABD, angle ABD = 180° − 90° − angle BAD.
2. Complementary pair at B: angle ABD + angle DBC = 90°, so angle DBC = angle BAD.
3. AA criterion, with the vertices written in matching order (A↔B, D↔D, B↔C).
4. Equal ratios, then cross-multiply: AD/BD = DB/DC gives BD² = AD × DC.
5. Area: BD is the height on base AC, so area = ½ × AC × BD.

## How each question maps

| Q | What it practises | Help given in the statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AA from two angles; third angle by angle sum; vertex order differs from reading order (flipped triangle) | two angles in each triangle |
| 2 | AA where the angles come from parallel lines and a common angle (hidden condition) | the parallel lines are marked |
| 3 | Ideas 1 and 2, with 56° first and then x° | the angle at A |
| 4 | Idea 3 alone, plus writing the ratios | the equal angle is given |
| 5 | Ideas 4 and 5 | the similarity is given |
| 6 | Ideas 1, 2 and 3 together | none: the equal angle must be found |
| 7 | Ideas 3 and 4 as a pair | the equal angle is given |
| 8 | Reverse: matching vertices not given; ideas 1 to 4 used to decide and then find DC | the similarity, but not the order |
| 9 | Target: everything, in exam wording | none |

Q3 to Q9 all use one figure, so the student's effort goes on the ideas, not on reading a new
diagram each time.

## What a fuller set on this topic would add

- More pairs (ideas 1+3, 2+4) before the all-together question.
- Hidden-condition versions: overlapping triangles, vertically opposite angles.
- Mixed items where SAS, SSS or "not similar" is the right answer.
- A one-line reflection after each question ("Why did AA fit here?").
