# Similar Triangles, Class 2: agreed brief and lesson route (draft for approval)

## Destination
Students prove and use the Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT). They use its converse. They decide whether
AA, SSS or SAS proves two triangles similar, write the similarity statement in the correct vertex order,
and use it to find lengths and angles and to prove simple results.

## Scope
- **Included:** all of the old Part 2: BPT (NCERT Th 6.1), converse (6.2), AAA/AA (6.3), SSS (6.4),
  SAS (6.5), choosing a criterion, applications (NCERT Examples 1 to 8, Exercises 6.2 and 6.3 problem types).
- **Excluded:** areas of similar triangles and Pythagoras (not in the CBSE Class 10 syllabus list; evidence in the thread).
- **Proofs (CBSE wording):** BPT is "Prove". The converse and the three criteria are "Motivate".
  Plan: full proof of BPT; for the converse and the criteria, a discovery activity first, then a short
  proof under "Why it works". The fc marks which proofs are examinable.

## Route (one document, taught over as many classes as needed)
1. **Recap of Class 1.** Short check: correspondence, scale factor, the definition of similar triangles. Rewritten questions.
2. **A parallel line cuts both sides in the same ratio.** Measuring activity, then BPT with the area proof.
   Worked examples (NCERT Ex 1, Ex 2, one direct case). Exercise 2A.
3. **The converse.** Activity: equal ratios on two sides, then check if the line is parallel. Theorem 2,
   short proof. Worked examples (NCERT Ex 3, one more). Exercise 2B.
4. **Why criteria are needed.** Counterexamples: a square and a rhombus (equal angles, different shapes for
   quadrilaterals); triangles show this does not happen. The six checks, and why fewer are enough.
5. **AA.** Construction activity (same angles, different sizes). Theorem 3, AA corollary. Worked examples
   (NCERT Ex 4, one setting). Exercise 2C.
6. **SSS.** Activity with side lists. Theorem 4. Worked examples (NCERT Ex 5). Exercise 2D.
7. **SAS.** Activity, then the non-included-angle counterexample figure. Theorem 5. Worked examples (NCERT Ex 6). Exercise 2E.
8. **Choosing the criterion.** A sort of mixed cases, including "not enough information". The proof frame
   (name the triangles, list the facts, name the criterion, write the statement in order).
9. **Applications.** Shadows (NCERT Ex 7, in an Indian setting such as a temple tower or a school flagpole),
   medians (NCERT Ex 8), trapezium diagonals. Exercise 2F, mixed.
10. **Things to Remember**, between grey rules.
11. **If you are ready for more** (reserve bank, answers in the fc only).

## Rules applied (same as Perimeter and Area Class 2)
- Every worked example: rough diagram with the givens marked and "?" on the unknown, **Given:**, **To find:**,
  **Solution.** with a short reason for each step, **Answer:** with units.
- Every exercise is rewritten. Each set tests the idea from several angles: find, work backwards,
  true/false or "Is she right?" (not always "No"), find the mistake, draw the figure as a marked part,
  "what if", real-life. At most one plain substitution question per set, and it comes first.
- Every question states what to find. Easy to hard. Indian settings in plain English. Whole-number answers
  unless the decimal is the point.
- House design: Century Schoolbook, black on white, grey figures drawn to true measurements, no colour
  words, heading "Similar Triangles" left and "Class 2" right with one thin rule.
- One source with `\ifteacher` for both copies. The fc has a short teacher overview, answers with a rough
  answer diagram in small grey text, and a "Note for the teacher" only where it is really needed, in formal wording.
