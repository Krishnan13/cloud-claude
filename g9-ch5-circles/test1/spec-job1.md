Q1. In a circle with centre $O$, chords $AB$ and $CD$ are equal and $\angle OAB=55^\circ$. What is $\angle COD$? \marks{1}
Options: $55^\circ$ / $70^\circ$ / $110^\circ$ / $125^\circ$. No student figure.
Solution: **(b) $70^\circ$.** 1. $OA=OB$ (radii), so $\angle OBA=\angle OAB=55^\circ$. 2. $\angle AOB=180^\circ-55^\circ-55^\circ=70^\circ$ (angle sum of a triangle). 3. $\angle COD=\angle AOB=70^\circ$ (equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre, Theorem 1). Figure: circle, chords AB and CD (equal length, apart), radii OA, OB, OC, OD, 55° marked at A.

Q2. A chord of length $20$~cm is drawn in a circle of radius $26$~cm. What is the distance of the chord from the centre? \marks{1}
Options: $6$~cm / $16$~cm / $24$~cm / $28$~cm.
Solution: **(c) $24$~cm.** 1. $OM\perp AB$, so $AM=10$~cm (the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, Theorem 4). 2. $OM^2=26^2-10^2=676-100=576$, so $OM=24$~cm (Pythagoras theorem). Figure: chord AB, OM perpendicular, OA, labels 26 on OA, 10 on AM.

Q3. Which statement about a circle with centre $O$ is \textbf{false}? \marks{1}
(a) The line joining $O$ to the midpoint of a chord (not a diameter) is perpendicular to the chord. (b) The perpendicular from $O$ to a chord bisects the chord. (c) The perpendicular bisector of every chord passes through $O$. (d) Every line through $O$ bisects every chord of the circle.
Solution: **(d).** A line through $O$ that is slanted to a chord (not a diameter) meets the chord away from its midpoint. Statements (a), (b) and (c) are Theorems 3 and 4 and their consequence. Figure: chord AB below O, a slanted line through O meeting AB at a point P away from the midpoint.

Q4. Fill in the blank: The number of circles that pass through three points lying on one straight line is \rule{1.5cm}{0.4pt}. \marks{1}
Solution: **$0$ (none).** The perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $BC$ are parallel, so they never meet. No point is at the same distance from all three points. (Theorem 5 needs three non-collinear points.) Figure: three points A, B, C on a line with two parallel dashed perpendicular bisectors.

Q5. A circle has radius $10$~cm. It has two chords of lengths $12$~cm and $18$~cm. Which chord is nearer the centre? \marks{1}
Options: The $12$~cm chord / The $18$~cm chord / Both are at the same distance / It cannot be decided without more information.
Solution: **(b).** Of two chords of a circle, the longer chord is nearer the centre (Theorem 8). Figure: circle with a longer chord near O and a shorter chord farther from O, perpendiculars from O.

Q6. Assertion--reason. Directions printed once before the items (in small italic): "Choose: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A. (c) A is true, but R is false. (d) A is false, but R is true."
Assertion (A): In a circle with centre $O$, $OM\perp AB$, where $AB$ is a chord and $M$ lies on $AB$. If $AM=5$~cm, then $AB=10$~cm.
Reason (R): The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. \marks{1}
Solution: **(a).** $OM\perp AB$, so $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ (Theorem 4). So $AB=2\times5=10$~cm. R is the reason for A. Figure: chord AB, OM perpendicular, AM = 5 cm marked, tick marks on AM and MB.

Q7. In a circle with centre $O$, chord $AB=10$~cm. $P$ is a point on $AB$ with $AP=4$~cm. Meena writes: ``$OP$ is perpendicular to $AB$, because the line joining the centre to a point of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.''
\pc{a}{What is wrong in Meena's reasoning?\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Which point $Q$ of $AB$ must be joined to $O$ so that $OQ\perp AB$? How far is $Q$ from $A$?\marks{1}}
Solution: (a) Theorem 3 is true only for the line from the centre to the \emph{midpoint} of the chord. $P$ is not the midpoint: $AP=4$~cm but $PB=6$~cm. So $OP$ is not perpendicular to $AB$. (b) $Q$ is the midpoint of $AB$ (Theorem 3), so $AQ=\dfrac{10}{2}=5$~cm. \Ans $Q$ is the midpoint of $AB$; $AQ=5$~cm. Figure: chord AB, P near A, Q at midpoint, OQ with right-angle mark, OP dashed.

Q8. Divya draws a circle with centre $O$ for a rangoli. She marks $10$ dots on the circle at equal distances and numbers them $1$ to $10$ in order. She joins dot $1$ to dot $4$, and dot $6$ to dot $9$ with straight lines.
STUDENT FIGURE (needed): circle radius 1.6, centre O dot labelled O, 10 dots at angles 90 - 36*(k-1) degrees for k=1..10, numbers 1..10 placed just outside each dot (radius 1.9), chord from dot 1 to dot 4 and chord from dot 6 to dot 9 drawn thick.
\pc{a}{Find the angle that the line from dot $1$ to dot $4$ subtends at $O$.\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Show that the two lines are equal in length.\marks{1}}
Solution: \Given 10 equally spaced dots on a circle with centre $O$; chords from dot 1 to dot 4 and from dot 6 to dot 9. \Tofind (a) the angle at $O$; (b) show the chords are equal. 1. The 10 dots divide the full angle $360^\circ$ at $O$ into 10 equal angles of $\dfrac{360^\circ}{10}=36^\circ$. 2. From dot 1 to dot 4 there are 3 gaps, so the angle is $3\times36^\circ=108^\circ$. 3. From dot 6 to dot 9 there are also 3 gaps, so that angle is also $108^\circ$. 4. The two chords subtend equal angles at the centre, so they are equal (Theorem 2). \Ans (a) $108^\circ$; (b) the chords are equal. Small figure: same as student figure, small, with radii to dots 1,4,6,9.

Q9. Chords $PQ$ and $RS$ of a circle with centre $O$ are each $16$~cm from $O$, and $PQ=24$~cm.
\pc{a}{Find $RS$. Give the reason.\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Find the radius of the circle.\marks{1}}
Solution: \Given $OM\perp PQ$, $ON\perp RS$, $OM=ON=16$~cm, $PQ=24$~cm. \Tofind $RS$ and the radius. 1. $PQ$ and $RS$ are equidistant from $O$, so $RS=PQ=24$~cm (Theorem 7). 2. $PM=\dfrac{24}{2}=12$~cm (Theorem 4). 3. $OP^2=OM^2+PM^2=256+144=400$, so $OP=20$~cm (Pythagoras theorem). \Ans $RS=24$~cm; radius $20$~cm. Figure: two chords at equal distances, perpendiculars marked 16.

Q10. A chord of a circle is $18$~cm long and is $12$~cm from the centre. Find the diameter of the circle. Draw a rough diagram that shows the circle, its centre $O$, the chord $AB$ and the perpendicular $OM$ from $O$ to $AB$. Mark the given lengths on it. \marks{2}
Solution: \Given chord $AB=18$~cm, $OM\perp AB$, $OM=12$~cm. \Tofind the diameter. 1. $AM=\dfrac{18}{2}=9$~cm (Theorem 4). 2. $OA^2=OM^2+AM^2=144+81=225$, so $OA=15$~cm (Pythagoras theorem). 3. Diameter $=2\times15=30$~cm. \Ans $30$~cm. Figure: the rough diagram described.
