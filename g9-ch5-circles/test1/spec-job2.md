Q11. $AB$ and $CD$ are parallel chords of a circle with centre $O$ and radius $25$~cm. $AB=40$~cm and $CD=30$~cm.
\pc{a}{Find the distance of each chord from $O$.\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Find the distance between the chords when they lie on the same side of $O$.\marks{1}}
\pc{c}{Find the distance between the chords when they lie on opposite sides of $O$.\marks{1}}
For (b) and (c), draw a rough diagram of each case. Show the circle, its centre $O$, both chords, and one line through $O$ perpendicular to both chords.
Solution: \Given radius $25$~cm, $AB\parallel CD$, $AB=40$~cm, $CD=30$~cm; $OM\perp AB$, $ON\perp CD$. \Tofind $OM$, $ON$ and the distance $MN$ in both cases. 1. $AM=20$~cm and $CN=15$~cm (Theorem 4). 2. $OM^2=25^2-20^2=625-400=225$, so $OM=15$~cm (Pythagoras theorem). 3. $ON^2=25^2-15^2=625-225=400$, so $ON=20$~cm. 4. $M$, $O$, $N$ lie on one line perpendicular to both chords. Same side: $MN=ON-OM=20-15=5$~cm. 5. Opposite sides: $MN=OM+ON=15+20=35$~cm. \Ans (a) $AB$ is $15$~cm and $CD$ is $20$~cm from $O$; (b) $5$~cm; (c) $35$~cm. Figures: TWO small figures side by side (same side; opposite sides). Radius 25 scaled to 1 cm: AB at distance 0.6 from O, CD at distance 0.8.

Q12. $AB$ and $CD$ are equal chords of a circle with centre $O$. Prove that $\angle OAB=\angle OCD$. \marks{3}
Student figure: circle radius 1.5, chords AB (around angles 200° to 280°) and CD (around 20° to 100°), equal length, radii OA, OB, OC, OD drawn.
Solution: \Given $AB$ and $CD$ are chords of a circle with centre $O$, $AB=CD$. \Toprove $\angle OAB=\angle OCD$. \Constr join $OA$, $OB$, $OC$, $OD$. Proof: In $\tr OAB$ and $\tr OCD$: 1. $OA=OC$ (radii). 2. $OB=OD$ (radii). 3. $AB=CD$ (given). 4. So $\tr OAB\cong\tr OCD$ (SSS rule). 5. So $\angle OAB=\angle OCD$ (corresponding parts of congruent triangles). \Hp

Q13. Three houses $A$, $B$ and $C$ stand in a village. $AB=300$~m, $BC=400$~m and $\angle ABC=90^\circ$. The panchayat wants to dig a well at a point $W$ that is at the same distance from all three houses.
\pc{a}{Use the scale $1$~cm $=50$~m. Draw $AB=6$~cm. At $B$, draw $\angle ABC=90^\circ$ and mark $C$ with $BC=8$~cm. Join $AC$.\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Construct the perpendicular bisectors of $AB$ and $BC$. Mark the point $W$ where they meet.\marks{1}}
\pc{c}{Measure $WA$. Find the real distance of the well from each house.\marks{1}}
Solution: \Given $AB=300$~m, $BC=400$~m, $\angle B=90^\circ$; scale $1$~cm $=50$~m. \Tofind the position of $W$ and its distance from each house. 1. $AB=\dfrac{300}{50}=6$~cm and $BC=\dfrac{400}{50}=8$~cm on paper. 2. Every point on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ is equally far from $A$ and $B$; every point on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$ is equally far from $B$ and $C$. So $W$ is equally far from $A$, $B$ and $C$. It is the centre of the only circle through $A$, $B$ and $C$ (Theorem 5). 3. $W$ falls on $AC$, at its midpoint. $AC^2=6^2+8^2=100$, so $AC=10$~cm and $WA=5$~cm (check by measuring). 4. Real distance $=5\times50=250$~m. \Ans the well is $250$~m from each house. Figure: right triangle ABC (right angle at B), dashed perpendicular bisectors meeting at W, the midpoint of AC; the circle through A, B, C dashed.

Q14. A circle has centre $O$ and radius $30$~cm. Chord $AB=48$~cm and chord $CD=36$~cm.
\pc{a}{Without calculating, say which chord is nearer the centre. Give the reason.\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Find the distance of each chord from $O$, and check your answer to (a).\marks{2}}
Solution: \Given radius $30$~cm, $AB=48$~cm, $CD=36$~cm; $OM\perp AB$, $ON\perp CD$. \Tofind which chord is nearer $O$; $OM$ and $ON$. 1. $AB>CD$, so $AB$ is nearer the centre (Theorem 8). 2. $AM=24$~cm (Theorem 4); $OM^2=900-576=324$, so $OM=18$~cm. 3. $CN=18$~cm (Theorem 4); $ON^2=900-324=576$, so $ON=24$~cm. 4. $18<24$, so $AB$ is nearer. This agrees with (a). \Ans $AB$ is nearer; $OM=18$~cm, $ON=24$~cm. Figure: two chords (not parallel needed), perpendiculars from O, radius scaled 30 -> 1.0 (distances 0.6 and 0.8).

Q15.
\pc{a}{Prove that the perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.\marks{3}}
\pc{b}{Two circles have the same centre $O$. A straight line meets the bigger circle at $A$ and $D$, and the smaller circle at $B$ and $C$, in the order $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ (see the figure). Prove that $AB=CD$.\marks{2}}
Student figure (for b): two concentric circles radii 1.7 and 1.0, centre O; horizontal line y = -0.6 from left of big circle to right; points A, B, C, D where it meets the circles (A and D on big circle, B and C on small circle), labels above the line for B and C and above-left/above-right for A, D (outside circles where possible, never on lines).
Solution (a): \Given a circle with centre $O$, chord $AB$, $OM\perp AB$. \Toprove $AM=MB$. \Constr join $OA$ and $OB$. Proof: In $\tr OMA$ and $\tr OMB$: 1. $\angle OMA=\angle OMB=90^\circ$ (given). 2. $OA=OB$ (radii; hypotenuses). 3. $OM=OM$ (common side). 4. So $\tr OMA\cong\tr OMB$ (RHS rule). 5. So $AM=MB$ (corresponding parts of congruent triangles). \Hp Small figure.
Solution (b): \Given two circles with centre $O$; a line meets them at $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$ in this order. \Toprove $AB=CD$. \Constr draw $OM\perp AD$. Proof: 1. $AD$ is a chord of the bigger circle, so $AM=MD$ (Theorem 4). 2. $BC$ is a chord of the smaller circle, so $BM=MC$ (Theorem 4). 3. $AB=AM-BM$ and $CD=MD-MC$. 4. So $AB=CD$ (equals subtracted from equals). \Hp Small figure with OM and right-angle mark.

Q16. The front of a temple gate has an arch. The arch is part of a circle with centre $O$. The ends $A$ and $B$ of the arch are $16$~m apart. The highest point $T$ of the arch is directly above the midpoint $M$ of $AB$, and $TM=4$~m.
Student figure: arc of a circle from A to B over the top (circle radius 10 scaled to 0.17 per m: r = 1.7, centre O at (0,0), A=(-1.36,1.02), B=(1.36,1.02), T=(0,1.7), M=(0,1.02)); draw only the arc from A over T to B (angles from about 36.87° to 143.13°), the segment AB, and dashed TM. Mark 16 m below AB and 4 m beside TM. Do NOT draw O or the rest of the circle.
\pc{a}{Explain why the centre $O$ lies on the line $TM$.\marks{1}}
\pc{b}{Let the radius be $r$ m. Draw a rough diagram that shows the full circle, $O$, $A$, $B$, $M$ and $T$. Find $r$.\marks{2}}
\pc{c}{A straight iron bar $CD$ is fixed across the arch, parallel to $AB$ and $2$~m above it, with $C$ and $D$ on the arch. Find the length of the bar. Is your answer in agreement with Theorem 8? Explain.\marks{2}}
Solution: \Given arc through $A$, $T$, $B$; $AB=16$~m; $M$ midpoint of $AB$; $TM\perp AB$; $TM=4$~m. \Tofind (a) why $O$ is on $TM$; (b) radius $r$; (c) length of $CD$. (a) $M$ is the midpoint of chord $AB$, so $OM\perp AB$ (Theorem 3). $TM$ is also perpendicular to $AB$ at $M$. Only one line through $M$ is perpendicular to $AB$, so $O$ lies on line $TM$. (b) 1. $OT=OA=r$ (radii), so $OM=r-4$. 2. $AM=8$~m. 3. In right $\tr OMA$: $r^2=(r-4)^2+8^2=r^2-8r+16+64$. 4. So $8r=80$ and $r=10$~m. $OM=6$~m. (c) 1. The bar is $2$~m above $AB$, so its distance from $O$ is $6+2=8$~m; let $N$ be its midpoint, $ON\perp CD$. 2. $CN^2=10^2-8^2=36$, so $CN=6$~m (Pythagoras theorem); $CD=2\times6=12$~m (Theorem 4). 3. The bar ($12$~m) is shorter than $AB$ ($16$~m) and farther from $O$ ($8$~m against $6$~m). This agrees with Theorem 8: the longer chord is nearer the centre. \Ans (b) $10$~m; (c) $12$~m; yes. Figure: full circle r=1.0 (scale 0.1 per m), O, chord AB at height 0.6 above O, CD at 0.8, T at top, OM, ON.

Q17. Case study. The sports ground of a school is a circle with centre $O$ and radius $29$~m. A flag post stands at $O$. For sports day, the PE teacher draws straight chalk lines from edge to edge of the ground as running tracks. Track $AB$ is $42$~m long.
Student figure: circle radius 1.6, centre O with a small flag (a short vertical line with a small triangle) labelled "flag post O", chord AB drawn at the correct distance (20/29 of radius = 1.10 from O) labelled A and B outside the circle.
\pc{i}{Find the distance of track $AB$ from the flag post.\marks{1}}
\pc{ii}{Track $CD$ is also $42$~m long. Without calculating, write the distance of $CD$ from the flag post. Name the theorem you used.\marks{1}}
\pc{iii}{Compare $\angle AOB$ and $\angle COD$. Give the reason.\marks{1}}
\pc{iv}{Track $EF$ is $40$~m long. Is $EF$ nearer to the flag post or farther from it than $AB$? Find its distance from the flag post.\marks{1}}
Solution: \Given radius $29$~m; $AB=CD=42$~m; $EF=40$~m. (i) $AM=21$~m (Theorem 4); $OM^2=29^2-21^2=841-441=400$, so $OM=20$~m. (ii) $20$~m: equal chords are equidistant from the centre (Theorem 6). (iii) $\angle AOB=\angle COD$: equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre (Theorem 1). (iv) $EF<AB$, so $EF$ is farther (Theorem 8). Half of $EF$ is $20$~m; $d^2=841-400=441$, so $d=21$~m. \Ans (i) $20$~m; (ii) $20$~m; (iii) equal; (iv) farther, $21$~m. Figure: circle, AB and CD at distance 20 (scaled), EF at 21, perpendiculars.
